# entropyx-survey

## Design Context

### The one idea

**Your repository, surveyed — every file placed on a terrain of risk you can walk.**

Everything on screen serves that sentence. entropyx already produces the measurements; this
interface's only job is to make 4,000 files legible as landscape instead of as a table.

### Users

Engineers, tech leads, and on-call responders reading a codebase they did not fully write.
They arrive with a question — *what changed, what's fragile, who owns it, what's rotting
quietly* — and they arrive under time pressure, often mid-incident. They are technical, they
distrust dashboards that assert without showing evidence, and they will stop trusting the
tool the instant a number looks made up.

Context of use: a laptop, daytime, often with someone else looking over their shoulder.
That is why the theme is **light** — this is a document you point at and discuss, not a
console you stare into alone at 2am.

### Brand personality

**Surveyed. Precise. Weathered.**

entropyx's own thesis is "measurements, not opinions" and "honest emptiness — when we don't
know, we return zero and say so." The interface inherits that: it must feel like a printed
field survey — authoritative because it shows its work and its margins, never because it
looks confident.

Credibility is the actual design problem here. Proof over adjectives. The determinism digest,
the phase timings, and the "0 blamed / 119 files" style counts are not debug output — they
are the trust surface, and they get designed, not hidden.

### Aesthetic direction

**Cartographic survey.** The codebase is terrain. Composite score reads as elevation via a
hypsometric tint ramp; directories are regions; the seven metric axes are a terrain profile;
tq1 events are survey marks plotted in time. Coordinate gutters, tick marks, a real map
legend, sheet metadata in the margin.

Palette derives from printed topographic quadrangle sheets — warm vellum paper, brown ink,
sienna contours, hypsometric green→ochre→umber for elevation, and a single survey-blue used
*only* for selection and interaction. Not decorative; every colour encodes a value.

Icons are cartographic symbols — triangulation station, fault trace, confluence, benchmark,
spot height — drawn as small inline SVG at text scale. They mark things on a map. They are
never large rounded badges sitting above headings.

**Anti-references:** observability dashboards (Datadog/Grafana dark + neon), generic admin
card grids, anything with a purple-to-blue gradient, glassmorphism, sparkline decoration.

**Type:** Barlow Condensed for map lettering and headings (condensed grotesque, the register
of real sheet labels), Archivo for body and UI, Spline Sans Mono with tabular figures for all
measured values. Numbers must align in columns — they are data, not text.

### Design principles

1. **Every colour encodes a value.** If a hue does not mean a number or a class, it does not
   ship. The accent blue is reserved for "you selected this."
2. **Show the margin of the measurement.** Counts, timings, digest, and honest zeros are
   first-class content. A blank region says *why* it is blank.
3. **Motion is the data arriving.** Cells wash in as rows stream and re-tint when weights
   change. Nothing animates decoratively; nothing bounces.
4. **The wait is a screen, not a spinner.** A scan runs 2s–12min and the cost is real and
   interesting. Phase telemetry teaches where the time goes.
5. **Left-aligned, asymmetric, gutter-anchored.** A survey sheet has margins and registration
   marks. Nothing is centred for comfort.

### Accessibility

Hypsometric tint is a sequential ramp, so it must never be the *only* channel — elevation is
always also stated numerically, and signal class always carries its symbol, not just its
colour. Ramp steps are checked for luminance separation so the terrain stays readable in
greyscale and with common colour-vision deficiencies. Full `prefers-reduced-motion` support:
the unfold becomes an instant paint, not a slower one.
